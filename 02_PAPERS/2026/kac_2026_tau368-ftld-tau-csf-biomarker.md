# Study question and hypothesis
- Primary question: Can the ratio of phosphorylated tau (p-tau181, p-tau212) to tau368 (a C-terminally truncated MTBR-region tau fragment) improve CSF biomarker discrimination of autopsy-confirmed FTLD-tau from FTLD-TDP and other non-tauopathies?
- Stated hypothesis: Combining p-tau phosphorylation biomarkers with tau368 as an intra-individual "controller" for tau pathology burden should improve diagnostic sensitivity for FTLD-tau, where fold-changes of p-tau are smaller than in AD.
- Study type: Retrospective biomarker validation in an autopsy-confirmed human cohort; includes immunohistochemistry sub-study.

# Biological or clinical context
- Disease / exposure / process: Frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD-tau: PiD, PSP, CBD, AGD, GGT; FTLD-TDP types A–E); AD; α-synucleinopathy; healthy controls.
- Organism: Human
- Tissue / cell type: CSF (antemortem); postmortem frontal cortex and hippocampal CA1 (IHC sub-study).
- Model system: Autopsy-confirmed clinical cohort
- Cohort or population: Penn INDD biomarker cohort (n=176; AD n=52, FTLD-tau n=34, FTLD-TDP n=29, αSyn n=32, controls n=29). UCSD ADRC IHC sub-cohort (n=16, frontal cortex n=15, hippocampus n=14 sections).
- Relevant stage, age, sex, genotype, or disease severity: Symptomatic at CSF collection (mean disease onset 4.4 years prior). APOE ε4 status collected; genotyping of MAPT, GRN, C9orf72 performed. Age at CSF and sex included as covariates.

# Study design
- Experimental or observational unit: Individual participant (CSF sample)
- Unit of inference: Diagnostic group (AD, FTLD-tau, FTLD-TDP, αSyn, controls)
- Groups and sample size per group: AD n=52, FTLD-tau n=34 (PiD, CBD, AGD, GGT, PSP), FTLD-TDP n=29 (subtypes A–E, ALS with untypable TDP-43), αSyn n=32 (PD, PDD, DLB), controls n=29. ADNC-negative subcohort: n=106 (PSP excluded for main analyses → n=102).
- Biological replicates: 1 CSF sample per participant
- Technical replicates: Simoa assays run in singlicate with internal QC controls at run start and end
- Randomization: Not applicable (retrospective cohort)
- Blinding: IHC image analysis performed blinded to diagnosis and Braak stage
- Inclusion / exclusion criteria: Neuropathological diagnosis of AD (ADNC high/intermediate), FTLD-tau (tau inclusions by neuropathologic criteria), FTLD-TDP (subtypes A–E), αSyn (brainstem/limbic/diffuse Lewy bodies). Controls: MMSE ≥28, CSF Aβ42/40 >0.072. PSP excluded from ADNC-negative subcohort main analyses (sensitivity analyses provided).
- Batch structure: Simoa HD-X analyses performed at University of Gothenburg; p-tau181 and Aβ42/40 on Fujirebio Lumipulse G1200 at Penn Biomarker Research Lab; t-tau on Luminex xMAP at Penn. Cross-platform batch effects not formally addressed.
- Longitudinal, repeated-measures, paired, or donor structure: Single antemortem CSF sample per participant; time from CSF to autopsy not reported.
- Controls and comparators: Healthy controls (CSF biomarker-negative, no cognitive impairment) as reference group; t-tau and p-tau/t-tau ratios as comparators.

# Experimental methods
- Assay / platform: Simoa HD-X (tau368, p-tau212 — University of Gothenburg in-house assays); Fujirebio Lumipulse G1200 (p-tau181, Aβ42/40); Luminex xMAP (t-tau). IHC: Olympus VS200 Slide Scanner (20× objective), QuPath v0.7.0 quantification.
- Sample preparation: CSF: standardized LP collection, −80°C storage. IHC: FFPE sections 5 µm; heat antigen retrieval (sodium citrate pH 6.0, microwave 100°C 20 min); blocking 0.1% normal goat serum; primary anti-tau368 (mouse monoclonal in-house, 1:500) overnight 4°C; secondary Alexa Fluor 647 goat anti-mouse; co-stained with qFTAA (2.4 µM) and hFTAA (0.77 µM) luminescent conjugated oligothiophenes (LCOs).
- Dose, concentration, force, exposure, or perturbation: Not applicable (observational)
- Timing and sampling schedule: Antemortem CSF; postmortem tissue at autopsy. Time between CSF and autopsy not specified.
- Matrix / medium / substrate / environmental conditions: CSF (antemortem lumbar); FFPE postmortem brain tissue.
- Primary endpoint: AUC for discrimination of FTLD-tau from FTLD-TDP in the ADNC-negative subcohort.
- Secondary endpoints: Discrimination of AD from all non-tau groups; discrimination of FTLD-tau from all non-tau groups; correlation with brain tau/Aβ burden; clinical variant discrimination (bvFTD-tau vs bvFTD-TDP).
- QC criteria: Simoa: within-run CV 3.9–5% (p-tau212), 6% (tau368); between-run CV 11.3–13.6% (p-tau212), 6% (tau368). tau368 normalized to internal QC mean per plate.
- Failure or exclusion criteria: PSP excluded from ADNC-negative subcohort main analyses (sensitivity analyses including PSP reported in supplement). Listwise deletion for missing data in logistic models.
- Exact reusable parameters and source location: Simoa HD-X: p-tau212 diluted 10× in Tau 2.0 Sample Diluent (Quanterix #101556); tau368 run neat. Antibody details: tau368 capture = in-house anti-tau368-specific; detection = in-house anti-tau241–441 mAb library. Calibrators: p-tau212 = in vitro phosphorylated recombinant full-length tau-441 (#269022, Abcam); tau368 = in-house recombinant Tau1-368 protein.

# Computational and statistical methods
- Raw input: CSF biomarker concentrations (pg/mL) from Simoa/Lumipulse/Luminex
- Preprocessing: Log-transformation and scaling (Z-score) of biomarker values for logistic regression
- Normalization: tau368 normalized to plate internal QC mean
- Feature filtering: Not applicable (predefined biomarkers)
- Covariates and design formula: AD models: age at CSF, sex, APOE ε4. FTLD/ADNC-negative models: age at CSF, sex.
- Statistical test or model: Kruskal–Wallis and Mann–Whitney–Wilcoxon (pairwise group comparisons); logistic regression (log-transformed, scaled biomarkers, HR reported); Spearman correlation (biomarker vs pathology burden); Pearson correlation (within FTLD-tau group, tau burden associations); ROC/AUC with 2000-iteration bootstrapping; DeLong's test for AUC comparison; Hittner's method for comparing Pearson correlations.
- Multiple-testing correction: FDR correction across 7 CSF biomarkers (p-tau181, p-tau212, tau368, t-tau, p-tau181/tau368, p-tau212/tau368, tau368/t-tau). Threshold p=0.05.
- Effect-size reporting: Median fold change between groups; hazard ratios (HR) from logistic regression; AUC with 95% CI (bootstrapped); Pearson r with 95% CI.
- Batch handling: Not formally addressed across platforms; Simoa analyses centralized at Gothenburg.
- Validation strategy: All analyses conducted in full cohort; ADNC-negative subcohort analyses as primary sensitivity; PSP-inclusive analyses in supplement.
- External validation: No independent external cohort. Single-center CSF biobank (Penn INDD).
- Software and versions: QuPath v0.7.0 (IHC quantification). Statistical software not specified in full text.

# What is actually new?
- Biological novelty: First demonstration that p-tau/tau368 ratio improves diagnostic accuracy for FTLD-tau vs FTLD-TDP discrimination in an autopsy-confirmed cohort. tau368 functions as an intra-individual "controller" reflecting tangle-related pathology.
- Methodological novelty: Novel p-tau181/tau368 and p-tau212/tau368 ratio approach for FTLD biomarker discrimination, with mechanistic support from IHC showing tau368 localizes to NFTs and brain tau burden correlations.
- Dataset or resource novelty: Rare autopsy-confirmed Penn INDD cohort spanning FTLD subtypes with matched antemortem CSF and brain pathology scores.
- What was already known: tau368/t-tau ratio was previously shown to correlate with tau PET and discriminate AD from controls. p-tau181 and p-tau212 are established AD biomarkers. FTLD lacked robust fluid biomarkers. PSP has distinctively low soluble tau CSF levels.

# Strengths
- Design strength: Autopsy-confirmed diagnoses across multiple FTLD-tau subtypes and FTLD-TDP subtypes; matched antemortem CSF and postmortem brain pathology scores; IHC validation of tau368 immunoreactivity.
- Validation strength: Multiple biomarker comparators (p-tau181, p-tau212, t-tau, tau368/t-tau, p-tau181/t-tau); FDR correction; bootstrapped AUCs; DeLong's tests for direct AUC comparisons; sensitivity analyses (PSP-inclusive, ADNC co-pathology-adjusted).
- Reproducibility strength: Simoa assays centralized at Gothenburg with rigorous QC. Antibodies validated by mass spectrometry (cited prior publications). IHC protocol fully specified.
- Translational or ecological realism: CSF from living symptomatic patients; diagnoses confirmed at autopsy; FTD clinical variants analyzed separately.

# Limitations and bias risks
- Sample-size or power limitation: Total n=176; ADNC-negative subcohort n=102 after PSP exclusion; individual FTLD-tau subtypes have very small n (e.g., GGT, AGD). Subgroup (clinical variant) analyses are exploratory.
- Confounding: Time from CSF collection to autopsy not reported; preanalytical handling standardized but not fully characterized across the cohort.
- Batch or site effects: CSF biomarkers measured across three platforms (Simoa/Lumipulse/Luminex) at two sites; cross-platform batch correction not applied.
- Pseudoreplication risk: Single CSF sample per participant; no longitudinal samples.
- Leakage or overfitting risk: All analyses performed in the same cohort without external validation. AUC bootstrapping mitigates but does not eliminate optimism.
- Generalizability limitation: Predominantly symptomatic patients; no presymptomatic FTLD data. Racial and sociodemographic diversity limited. Some diseases missing (e.g., MSA). Findings may not generalize to neurology clinic populations.
- Missing controls: Multiple system atrophy not included. No CJD control group.
- Missing validation: No independent external cohort. Plasma biomarkers not evaluated in this study. Longitudinal biomarker dynamics not assessed.
- Author-stated limitations: Missing some diseases (MSA); small subgroup n; symptomatic-only cohort; limited racial diversity; plasma not evaluated.
- Additional limitations identified during review: p-tau212 and tau368 assays are in-house at Gothenburg and not commercially available; limits immediate transferability. Supplementary tables not inspected.

# Transferability to the user's work
- Directly transferable elements: Ratio approach (p-tau/tau368) applicable to any CSF collection with tau368 assay access. PSP caveat is directly actionable: exclude or stratify PSP in FTLD biomarker studies. Ang-1/Tie2 pathway not applicable here (that is Trivedi 2026).
- Elements requiring adaptation: In-house Simoa assays (p-tau212, tau368) are not commercially available; would require collaboration with Gothenburg lab or development of equivalent assays.
- Model, species, tissue, matrix, platform, or scale mismatch: Human CSF → plasma transfer not validated here. Findings in autopsy-confirmed cohort may not transfer to clinical diagnostic settings without prospective validation.
- Assumptions required for transfer: Assumes CSF biomarker availability; assumes tau pathology detected by PHF-1 IHC reflects relevant tau burden for biomarker correlation.
- Confidence: moderate (autopsy-confirmed, but single-site, no external validation, in-house assays)

# Practical implications
- Experimental implication: When designing FTLD biomarker studies, include tau368 as co-measurement with p-tau181/p-tau212. Exclude or separately analyze PSP. Target ADNC-negative enrichment for FTLD-tau vs FTLD-TDP comparisons.
- Analysis implication: p-tau/tau368 ratio outperforms p-tau/t-tau for FTLD-tau detection; use p-tau212/tau368 for FTLD-tau vs FTLD-TDP, p-tau181/tau368 for FTLD-tau vs all non-tau. FDR-correct across biomarker panel.
- Public dataset implication: Penn INDD data not publicly available; contact authors for collaboration.
- Biomarker or translational implication: p-tau212/tau368 is the top-performing single biomarker for FTLD-tau vs FTLD-TDP (AUC 0.95); potential enrichment tool for FTLD-tau clinical trials. Needs plasma validation.
- Grant or manuscript implication: Strong precedent for tau368 ratio in FTLD biomarker grant aims; cite alongside Irwin 2024 (TDP-43 loss-of-function fluid biomarker) for complementary coverage of FTLD-tau vs FTLD-TDP discrimination.

# Contradictions and links
- Supports: Irwin 2024 (need for FTLD-specific fluid biomarkers); Warmenhoven 2025 (p-tau platform comparisons, though AD-focused)
- Contradicts: Earlier reports suggesting p-tau181/t-tau as a useful FTLD-tau biomarker — this study finds p-tau181/t-tau does not survive FDR correction and underperforms vs p-tau181/tau368.
- Replicates: Previous findings that tau368 levels are stable across neurodegenerative disease groups and that tau368/t-tau is lower in FTLD; previous findings of elevated p-tau181 and p-tau212 in FTLD-tau.
- Methodologically comparable papers: Warmenhoven 2025 (ptau217 head-to-head, AD-focused); Irwin 2024 (cryptic HDGFL2, TDP-43 biomarker, FTLD-TDP-focused)
