# Current Literature Signals

This file contains dynamic priorities, not permanent rules. It should guide discovery and watchlists but must not override project-specific needs or evidence quality.

## Cross-cutting signals

### Single-cell, spatial, and atlas resources
Prioritize resources that combine cell-resolved profiling with tissue context, longitudinal or lifespan coverage, genotype, pathology, or independent validation. Searchable atlases and reusable reference layers are particularly valuable.

### Neurodegeneration biomarkers
Prioritize studies that compare diseases, biofluids, tissues, imaging, or molecular modalities and that distinguish disease-specific from transdiagnostic signatures. External cohorts and realistic clinical matrices matter more than sensitivity-only demonstrations.

### Biosensors and nanomaterial platforms
Favor studies that include realistic matrices, selectivity controls, stability, batch reproducibility, calibration, interference testing, independent samples, and translational constraints. Capture surface chemistry, dispersion QC, optical or electrochemical settings, and failure modes.

### Mechanobiology and injury models
Track work connecting pressure, impulse, stiffness, ECM remodeling, force signaling, confinement, barriers, glia, and neural phenotypes. Exact device calibration, waveform, matrix composition, timing, and cell state are essential.

### Multi-omics and AI
Track foundation models, graph methods, deep generative models, knowledge-graph workflows, and LLM-assisted analysis only when assumptions, missingness, batch handling, transferability, interpretability, and benchmarking are clear. Prefer independent comparisons and reproducible code.

### Public data and reusable methods
A small resource or methods paper may be more valuable than a prominent biological paper when it provides a reusable dataset, benchmark, atlas, protocol, software pipeline, or validation cohort.

## Domain modules

### Neurodegeneration, TBI, ALS, and AD/ADRD
Watch for:
- cell-type-resolved responses to injury and aging;
- tau, TDP-43, axonal injury, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and glial state transitions;
- genotype-by-injury interactions;
- longitudinal and cross-disease biomarker studies;
- organoid and human-derived models with reproducible injury calibration;
- public human transcriptomic, proteomic, and spatial datasets suitable for independent validation.

### SWCNT biosensors and spectral phenotyping
Watch for:
- rational or empirical corona-phase libraries;
- biofluid-compatible optical nanosensors;
- EEM or hyperspectral analysis;
- spectral feature extraction, calibration, drift correction, and batch robustness;
- machine-learning validation that is donor-aware and leakage-resistant;
- orthogonal chemical validation of sensor-corona interactions.

### Transcriptomics and computational biology
Watch for:
- donor-aware differential expression and time-course models;
- splicing and outlier detection;
- transposable-element and HERV quantification;
- cross-cohort harmonization;
- single-cell reference mapping and spatial integration;
- benchmark datasets that expose batch, missingness, or transferability limits.

### Mechanobiology and neural injury models
Watch for:
- calibrated force, pressure, impulse, and confinement systems;
- ECM stiffness, composition, and remodeling effects on neural and glial state;
- barrier (blood-brain, blood-nerve) models under mechanical and injury loads;
- force-signaling pathways linked to neurodegenerative phenotypes;
- reproducible device calibration, waveform, and matrix reporting;
- tissue-level and organoid disease models with mechanical readouts.

## Prioritization reminder
The best new paper is the one most likely to improve a current decision, reveal a reusable dataset, strengthen validation, expose a failure mode, or make an analysis more reproducible.
