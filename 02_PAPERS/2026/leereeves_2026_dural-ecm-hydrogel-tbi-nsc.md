# What is actually new?
- Biological novelty: Dural ECM specifically (not generic brain ECM) as a TBI hydrogel scaffold; MBV characterization from dural ECM and distinction from exosomes is a new biological finding within this biomaterial.
- Methodological novelty: Decellularized dural ECM hydrogel (porcine source); paired proteomics + MBV characterization + CCI in vivo + in vitro mechanistic validation in a single study.
- Dataset or resource novelty: 559-protein dural ECM proteome as a characterization reference (if deposited in ProteomeXchange/PRIDE).
- What was already known: Decellularized ECM hydrogels (e.g., brain ECM, bladder ECM) can improve TBI outcomes; NSC transplantation supports TBI recovery; ECM-derived materials can modulate inflammation.

# Strengths
- Design strength: Multi-endpoint study (proteomics + in vitro + in vivo CCI + behavioral); tests hydrogel alone, NSC alone, and combination — enables isolation of ECM vs cellular contributions.
- Validation strength: CCI in vivo motor recovery as functional endpoint; in vitro oxidative stress as mechanistic corroboration; two independent in vitro assays.
- Reproducibility strength: Decellularization protocol is publishable and replicable; proteomic characterization provides detailed biomaterial QC.
- Translational or ecological realism: CCI is the most widely used preclinical TBI model; porcine dura is clinically available and translationally relevant (used in neurosurgery).

# Limitations and bias risks
- Sample-size or power limitation: Exact n per group Unknown; motor recovery studies in CCI are often underpowered for long-term or subtle recovery effects.
- Confounding: Dural ECM may retain growth factors, cytokines, or other bioactive molecules beyond the 559 proteins catalogued — residual decellularization byproducts could contribute to observed effects.
- Batch or site effects: Single-site (Imperial/UCL/KCL); no independent replication.
- Pseudoreplication risk: Litter effects in CCI mice not mentioned in abstract.
- Leakage or overfitting risk: Low for in vivo motor recovery; low for proteomics.
- Generalizability limitation: Male mice only (MeSH confirms Male); porcine ECM source — xenogeneic immunogenicity in clinical translation; single cortical region (CCI site).
- Missing controls: Sham surgery not confirmed in abstract; comparison to brain-derived ECM hydrogel absent.
- Missing validation: Independent site replication absent; long-term recovery data (>2 weeks post-TBI) Unknown; histological characterization of transplant site and graft survival Unknown.
