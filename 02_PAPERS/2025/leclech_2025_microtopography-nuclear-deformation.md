# Study question and hypothesis
- Primary question: How does anisotropic micro-scale substrate topography (basement-membrane-like) drive nuclear deformation, and via what forces?
- Stated hypothesis: Microgroove topography induces large-scale dynamic 3D nuclear deformations driven principally by cell–substrate adhesion (not cytoskeletal force), without major DNA damage.
- Study type: In vitro mechanobiology (microfabricated substrates + live/fixed imaging + AFM).

# What is actually new?
- Biological novelty: Topography- (not whole-cell-confinement-) driven nuclear deformation via adhesion stresses, dynamic and DNA-damage-sparing.
- Methodological novelty: Microgroove substrate as a functional nuclear-mechanics diagnostic for laminopathies.
- What was already known: Whole-cell confinement deforms nuclei and can cause DNA damage; here topography-driven, adhesion-led, reversible.
